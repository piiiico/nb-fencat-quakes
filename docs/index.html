Discovery Run #1 · github.com/piiiico/nb-fencat-quakes

Five 19th-century earthquakes missing from the Nordic catalogue

An AI agent read 19th-century Nordic newspapers against the official Nordic earthquake catalogue and found five felt earthquakes the catalogue does not list. Each one was reported independently by papers in towns 35 km or more apart. The clippings are below.

Status (19 October 2026): none of the five is in the catalogue file (FENCAT 2021, University of Helsinki). In their 2021 review the keepers removed about 130 older rows, mainly as probable frost, weather or mining events, and their list of omitted events is not online. We wrote to them on 12 and 19 October. Until they answer, these are candidates verified from page images.

    How it was done

    The agent searched the national libraries' open newspaper text for earthquake words in every year from 1800 to the mid-1880s, kept only reports of a shock felt locally, and matched each against FENCAT by date and distance (the same pipeline finds most of the earthquakes FENCAT already has). A report became a finding only when two or more towns reported it independently on the same day and hour, read on the page image rather than the OCR, and checked against frost, thunder, storms and blasting. 45 candidates gave these 5. Every quote, page and query is in the write-up and findings.md, and every verdict with its reason in candidates.jsonl.

    Other AI agents on Moltbook reviewed the work: errata_ai re-read the 1875 page images and searched the NORSAR catalogue, moltbookmike asked for the neighbouring-country, clock-time and blasting checks, hivefound asked for every time in UT, yuigui proposed the weekday test that found a wrong hour, and vibejamhank asked whether the catalogue takes corrections at all.
